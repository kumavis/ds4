### Bottom line

**The current prefill sweep cannot benefit from biased routing. Do not build
prefill biasing as a router change.**

V4.1's layer-major prefill loads each layer **whole** into the two-layer
staging buffer, independent of routing:
- `ds41_stream_layer_start`, `cuda_stream_v41_prepare_layer` and
  `cuda_stream_v41_load_layer` handle it;
- resident experts are copied from the cache and the other experts are read
  from disk;
- the load is prepared before (and overlapped with) the layer's router.

**The measurement matches.** The session read about 12,300 experts (about
216 GiB) per chunk lane, whatever the chunk's size:

| turn | new tokens | experts read |
|---|---:|---:|
| 5 | 256 | 12,357 |
| 3 | 1,147 | 12,212 |
| 7 | 952 | 12,399 |
| two-lane turns | 2.6k-4.4k | 22.9k-23.2k |

That is 40 x 384 = 15,360 minus the roughly 3,000 resident experts that were
copied, not the routed union. Server counters: 560 full-layer loads,
3,765 GiB; routing never decided what was read.

**What biasing would save if the sweep loaded only the routed union.** This is
modelled from the trace and is not built. A union-only load would itself be
exact (no routing change). Against the measured whole-layer reads of about
12,300 experts per lane, the unbiased union's non-resident experts are:
- 3,656 for about 256-row chunks: -70% (modelled);
- 5,138 for 513-1,023 rows: -58%;
- 6,719 for 1,024-2,048 rows: -42%.

Cache-Prior on top of a union load would cut the remaining reads by:
- 1-2k-row chunks: a further 19% (lambda 0.05) / 29% (0.09) / 36% (0.2) with
  keep 3;
- about 256-row chunks: 27% / 37% / 45%.

**Keep 3 does not leave the union nearly unchanged at 2k rows.** On the warm
1-2k chunks, the union shrinks from 10,946 to 9,148 experts at lambda 0.09
(-16%), and the reads fall 29%. It pays at every chunk size traced. The
relative saving is largest for small appends.

**The cost in routing change** is larger than in decode: in prefill
row-layers, 46% (lambda 0.05) to 57% (0.09) of chosen sets change, with mean
drift 7.4% to 10.6% (keep 3). D16's decode figures were 33-40% and 4-5%.

**Recommendation.**
1. **Don't build prefill biasing as a router change.** It would save nothing
   with the current sweep.
2. **The bigger lever in this trace is the load step:** loading the routed
   union instead of whole layers. It is exact, it is modelled at -42% to -70%
   of prefill reads, and it lives in the sweep's load step (D17's area). It
   trades away the whole-layer load's head start: a union can only be loaded
   after that layer's router has run, so less of the read overlaps compute.
   The modelled times below do not include that.
3. **Prefill biasing is worth building only on top of a union-loading sweep**
   (about 19-37% fewer reads again).

### The trace (commit 43aa265)

`DS4_ROCM_ROUTE_TRACE=<path>`, off by default. It appends one record per
multi-row prefill routing call; DSpark verify rounds and image rows are
excluded. The record holds:
- the gating scores `s` of every row (fp16);
- the layer's selection bias (once);
- at layer 0 of each chunk, the SSD cache's residency (40 x 384).

The score readback drains the stream, so tracing slows prefill. It reads
scores only and changes no route.

**Code touched:**
- `ds41_route_batch`: one call after the unbiased routing;
- new `ds41_route_trace` and `ds41_rt_f16` in ds4.c;
- new host helper `ds4_gpu_stream_resident_mask` in
  `rocm/ds4_rocm_router.cuh`.

The sweep and the load step are untouched.

### The model (`scripts/d16b/union.c`, offline, CPU)

**Per chunk lane and layer:**
- each row's top-6 of `c = s + bias`, unbiased, and with `+lambda*R` for
  experts resident at the chunk start or in the row's unbiased top-keep
  (ties to the lower index);
- R = the layer's mean range over all earlier traced prefill rows, frozen per
  chunk group. The first group has no history, so each row uses its own
  range;
- the union over the lane's rows;
- **reads** = union experts not resident at the chunk start. In-chunk
  evictions are ignored; cohort lanes are counted separately, which gives an
  upper bound for a cohort that shares one union;
- swapped row-layers and drift as in D16.

The modelled time uses the measured 7.5 GiB/s, with 18,800,640 bytes per
expert.

**Not in the trace:**
- chunks of 2-4k rows: the server chunks at 2,048, so a 2-4k append is a
  two-lane cohort;
- appends of under 256 rows;
- the decode rows' ranges, which would also feed R in the recipe.

### Caveats (all model, not measurement)

- The reads ignore in-chunk eviction, and per-expert reads may run at a
  different rate than whole-layer reads. The overlap that the whole-layer
  load gets (the next layer read during compute) is not modelled.
- Residency comes from an unbiased run. Under biased decode the resident set
  would differ.
- Cohort lanes are modelled with separate unions.
