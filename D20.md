**Reading:**
- The two expert kernels are 99% of the routed-MoE time at every size. Sorting,
  tile building, quantisation, the sum, launch count and host work are under
  1% together. There is no launch, sync or gather waste to remove first.
- **The down projection is the larger cost:** 62% at 2,048 rows, 69% at 325,
  although it has half of gate/up's FLOPs.
- This matches D17's stage figure: 556.7 ms × 40 = 22.3 s per 2,048-row
  sweep, against their FFN 20.5-23.1 s. That figure includes the shared expert,
  which is not in this profile.

**What the kernels do** (read in `rocm/ds4_rocm_moe.cuh`):
- **Arithmetic:** activations are quantised to Q8_K (int8 with a float scale
  per 256 elements). Each kernel dots MXFP4 weight rows (e2m1 values, an 8-bit
  power-of-two scale per 32) against them in integer arithmetic per block.
- **Layout:**
  - each tile is 8 pairs of one expert;
  - eight lanes split an output row's K blocks, then a quarter-warp float
    reduction finishes;
  - gate/up fuse SwiGLU and the route weight into their epilogue.

  No matrix cores are used: this is a matvec-style design with 8-token
  reuse.
- **Why down is slow (from the code; not measured inside the kernel):**
  - its K is 2,304 = 9 Q8_K blocks, but the kernel stages at most 8 blocks of
    activations in LDS (`staged = midq_blocks <= 8`). So down always runs the
    unstaged helper, which reads activations from global memory per lane;
  - nine blocks over eight lanes leave 7 of 8 lanes idle in the second
    iteration (9/16 = 56% lane use);
  - gate/up (K 5,120 = 20 blocks) is staged.
- **Not separable without instrumenting inside the kernels:** weight decode
  (MXFP4 nibble to integer) and the dot loop share one inner loop.

### Achieved throughput against the device

**Work** (2 FLOP per multiply-add):
- gate and up: 2 × 2 × 5,120 × 2,304 = 47.19 MFLOP per (row, expert) pair;
- down: 23.59 MFLOP per pair;
- total: 70.78 MFLOP per pair.
- Pairs = 6 × rows. At 2,048 rows that is 12,288 pairs: **0.87 TFLOP per
  layer.** The hypothesis of 0.6 TFLOP was low; the order of magnitude is
  right.

| rows | gate/up achieved | down achieved | whole routed MoE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2,048 | 2.84 TFLOP/s | 0.83 | 1.56 |
| 1,097 | 2.68 | 0.64 | 1.29 |
| 325 | 2.25 | 0.48 | 1.00 |

**Device figures** (all measured here, `tools/d20/peak.cpp`,
`/data/ds4-bench/d20-peak.log`; no vendor peak was verified from the
installed documentation):
- HIP reports "AMD Radeon 8060S Graphics", gfx1151, 20 multiprocessors (on
  this RDNA part I read that as 20 WGPs = 40 CUs; an inference, not
  verified), clock 2.9 GHz, wave 32.
- **Matrix-core ceiling, registers only:** WMMA f16→f32 **52.4 TFLOP/s**,
  WMMA iu8→i32 **51.6 TOP/s**. That is 8 independent accumulators per wave
  and 320 blocks of 4 waves, so no memory traffic.
- **hipBLASLt bf16** (bf16 in, f32 compute, bf16 out, heuristic algorithm 0):
  - 4096³: 23.8 TFLOP/s;
  - M × 2304 × 5120 (one expert's gate or up): 30.9 TFLOP/s at M = 2,048,

## Plan (cheapest first; each behind its own switch, off by default)

1. **Down kernel: stage all 9 Q8_K blocks.**
   - The activations of the down tile go into LDS (8 pairs × 9 blocks ×
     256 B = 18 KiB plus scales) instead of being read per lane from global
     memory. The 9-over-8 lane split stays.
   - Exact only if the staged and unstaged dot helpers are arithmetically
     identical. To be shown by a model-free kernel test (bitwise, random
     inputs) and the in-model gates; if they differ, it is declared fp-order.
   - Expected: down 348 → roughly 120-200 ms at 2,048 rows (estimate).
2. **The existing occupancy variants, measured in-model.** These are
   off-by-default and documented as bit-exact: `DS4_ROCM_ENABLE_MXFP4_TILE4`,
   `_ROW64`, `_TILE32`, `_LDSB` and `DS4_ROCM_MXFP4_DOWN_RGROUP`. No code; one
   run each.
3. **Matrix-core kernels for experts with at least 16 rows:** iu8 WMMA, int8
   activations (the existing Q8_K), and MXFP4 weights decoded to int8 in LDS.
   - e2m1 × 2 is an integer in [-12, 12], and the per-32 power-of-two scale
     stays outside.
   - The integer partial sum per 32-element sub-block equals the current
     kernel's. Applying the scales and summing in the current kernel's float
     order would make it bit-exact; otherwise it is fp-order and declared.
   - Experts with fewer rows keep the current kernels.
   - Gate/up first (fused SwiGLU epilogue as now), then down.
4. **hipBLASLt is not the first tool here:**
   - it cannot read MXFP4 on gfx1151;
   - expanding a layer's experts to bf16 per call writes about 384 × 3 ×
     11.8 M × 2 B, about 27 GB per layer (estimate), so per-expert expansion
     only fits the few large experts;
   - grouped bf16 GEMM over those (M ≥ 256 reaches 30+ TFLOP/s) is the
     fallback if step 3 cannot be made exact or fast. It would be fp-order
     (bf16 activations, not Q8).
5. The dense prefill GEMMs are out of scope until after the expert kernels,
   and I will ask first.

Every change gets:
- a model-free microbenchmark (time, and bitwise or difference against the
  current kernel);
- in-model gates: long c27a3b087fb5/255, DSpark json 207/305 and translate
  176/336, and the session reply tokens identical to the off twin, or the
  fp-order divergence measured;
- D14-harness TTFT and prefill tok/s for about 256, 1-2k and 2-4k appends at
  32k, and a cold 32k prefill.

### Questions answered from the code

**1. Do the decode-path expert kernels have the same mismatch? No.**
- Decode's down projection (`moe_down_mxfp4_sum6_qwarp32_kernel`, also used
  by D2's overlapped decode tail) stages nothing in LDS. One wave per output
  row: 16 lane pairs walk the 72 MXFP4 sub-blocks of K = 2,304 and read the
  six experts' Q8_K activations from global memory (cached).
  - 72 / 16 = 4.5 passes, so the fifth pass is half full: about 90% lane use.
- Decode gate/up (`moe_gate_up_mid_decode_mxfp4_qwarp32_kernel`, K = 5,120 =
  160 sub-blocks) divides evenly.
- No cheap structural fix of the step-1 kind is visible there. The 10% lane
  idling in down is an upper bound on what rebalancing could recover in that
  one kernel (estimate). I have not measured decode kernel times.

**2. The shared expert.**
- At 2,048 rows its three projections run `ds41_matmul_batch` →
  `metal_graph_matmul_plain_tensor` → `ds4_gpu_matmul_q8_0_tensor`. That is
  the MMQ dense Q8_0 path (`ds4_mmq_q8_0_dense_vec`, `cuda/mmq`), followed by
  SwiGLU, BF16 quantize and the down projection on the same path.
- Its work is 3 × 2 × 5,120 × 2,304 × 2,048 = 145 GFLOP per layer, a sixth of
  the routed experts' 0.87 TFLOP.
- Its time is not in my profile, and I have not measured it. Measuring it
  needs a mark either in `ds41_moe_batch` (D17's function) or in the dense
  Q8_0 matmul (my "ask first" item). A measurement-only mark in
  `ds4_gpu_matmul_q8_0_tensor` would answer it; I'll add it only if you agree.
